\section{Project Overview}
\sectionauthor{Annika Geiger}
\subsection{Project Description}
The COMETES ASM Team Project 2026 focuses on the development and integration of a mechatronic satellite subsystem. 
The system under study consists of a flat satellite (flatsat) to which additional components, namely an antenna and a radar module, 
are added. Based on these extensions, safety aspects must be analyzed and considered throughout the design process.

The project is carried out in collaboration with students from the French partner university SUPMECA in Paris. 
It combines mechanical design, system integration, simulation, and safety analysis within an international team environment.

\subsection{Project Tasks}

\subsubsection*{Step 1: Planning and Definition (Approx. 2 weeks)}
The first phase focuses on defining and structuring the work packages. The main tasks include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Planning and detailing work packages
	\item Comparing mechanical and motorized systems with emphasis on safety aspects
	\item Developing a locking system
	\item Creating the system model (2D or 3D CAD)
	\item Adapting the 3D structure into a 2D representation
	\item Identifying a suitable flatsat for payload integration
	\item Preparing the integration of the system into the flatsat
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Step 2: Development and Implementation (Approx. 10 weeks)}
In this phase, the defined work packages are carried out collaboratively in a French-German team. The tasks include:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Development and implementation of subsystem components
	\item Performing simulations and design validation
	\item Integration of the system into the flatsat
	\item Analysis and consideration of safety aspects
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Step 3: Final Presentation (Approx. 1 week)}
The final phase consists of presenting the project results:
\begin{itemize}
	\item Presentation of results in Esslingen and Paris
	\item Demonstration of design solutions and analysis outcomes
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Focus Areas}
\begin{itemize}
	\item Mechatronic system design
	\item Satellite system integration
	\item Safety analysis
	\item CAD modeling and simulation
	\item International teamwork
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Workload and Organization}
The total workload corresponds to approximately 240 hours per student. Weekly in-person meetings are required. 
The project builds upon previous work and includes interfaces with other project teams.

The implementation of the stakeholder and context diagrams in System Composer provided a structured representation of the system architecture, including the relevant stakeholders, external entities, system boundaries, and principal interactions. The component-based structure of System Composer enabled the individual elements to be represented clearly and organized within the overall system architecture.

However, transferring the handwritten diagrams into System Composer required several adaptations. In particular, relationships represented by informal arrows in the original diagrams had to be formalized using explicitly defined ports and connection directions. This was challenging for bidirectional or ambiguous interactions. Furthermore, reproducing the visual structure of the original diagrams required manual adjustment, as the automatic layout did not consistently preserve the intended arrangement.

The principal architectural information, including stakeholders, system boundaries, and general interaction flows, could be retained. However, qualitative information contained implicitly in the handwritten diagrams was more difficult to represent. Relationships describing concepts such as collaboration or feedback had to be simplified into directional connections, resulting in a partial loss of contextual meaning. Similarly, implicit assumptions and descriptive annotations could not always be transferred directly.

Overall, System Composer enabled a more formal and consistent representation of the system architecture but required the simplification and formalization of several elements from the original diagrams. Consequently, while the main structural information was preserved, some qualitative and implicit information was lost during the transformation.

\subsubsection{Modification of the Storage-Volume Requirement}

The initial storage-volume requirement specified a maximum volume of
$1.5\,\mathrm{U}$. During the CAD development, it was verified that the
collapsed antenna structure itself can be accommodated within this volume.
However, the additional motors and cable-based retraction mechanism require
further installation space. Consequently, the complete system cannot be
integrated within the original $1.5\,\mathrm{U}$ volume.

For the current design, the available storage volume was therefore increased
to $2\,\mathrm{U}$. The original requirement is retained as REQ-01 to preserve
requirement traceability, while the modified design requirement is defined as

\begin{quote}
	\textbf{REQ-01A:} The complete antenna system, including the deployment
	and retraction mechanisms, shall fit within a $2\,\mathrm{U}$ FlatSat box.
\end{quote}

The deviation from the initial requirement is therefore caused by the
additional installation volume required for the reusable motor-driven
retraction mechanism rather than by the collapsed antenna geometry itself.


\subsubsection{Project Constraints and Deliverables}

In addition to the technical system requirements, several organizational and
project-level constraints were identified during the kickoff meeting:

\begin{itemize}
	\item The primary project objective is the development of a ground-based
	test-bench model of the deployable antenna.
	
	\item Stakeholder and context diagrams shall be used to identify and
	refine the initial system requirements.
	
	\item A functional architecture shall be developed to describe the
	principal functions and kinematics of the antenna system.
	
	\item MATLAB/Simulink and Modelica models shall be developed and compared.
	
	\item Relevant antenna parameters shall be exchanged between the
	participating project teams, distinguishing between given boundary
	conditions and parameters resulting from the design process.
	
	\item Component selection shall be performed collaboratively between the
	participating teams.
	
	\item The interfaces to the FlatSat shall be defined as the FlatSat
	architecture becomes available.
	
	\item The project budget applies to the complete project, including the
	FlatSat and required components. Component selection shall therefore
	consider the available project resources.
	
	\item All developed models, CAD files, simulation results, and supporting
	documentation shall be handed over at the end of the project.
	
	\item The deliverables achievable within the current project phase shall
	be distinguished from work intended for subsequent project phases.
\end{itemize}
